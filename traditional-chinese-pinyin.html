Traditional Chinese to pinyin — readings without converting first

Pinyin for traditional characters

Traditional characters can take pinyin the same way simplified ones do. This page opens on a short traditional sample, so the readings you see first are for the characters as written, not for a simplified copy. The 简 and 繁 buttons can still rewrite the open text if you want the other set. Conversion is word by word. It is standard character mapping, not a separate Taiwan vocabulary list.

The sample is 今天天氣很好，我們去公園散步。圖書館旁邊是銀行。 我們 and 書 are enough for the script control to treat the text as traditional. 銀行 is the traditional form of 银行, and the reading is still yínháng. You do not have to convert to simplified before the pinyin appears.

How to get pinyin on traditional text

  1. Paste traditional characters into Text. The reading view keeps those characters and adds pinyin to the words.
  2. If the toggle shows 繁 pressed, the tool has detected traditional characters in the passage. You can leave it. Press 简 only if you want the text rewritten into simplified.
  3. Click a word for the definition and any other reading, the same way as on a simplified text.

What the conversion does

The converter is OpenCC, set for standard traditional, not Taiwan vocabulary. A simplified word such as 软件 becomes 軟件, not the Taiwan term 軟體. Place names and everyday words can still differ from the wording a teacher in Taipei would choose. The tool changes characters. It does not translate between regional word choices, and it does not add Zhuyin.

Going the other way, 繁 to 简, uses the matching simplified mapping. The open text is replaced. A correction you made is carried across when the characters of that word can be converted. If the length of the text changes in a way that breaks saved positions, reveals are dropped so a highlight does not stick to the wrong characters. Readings are then calculated again for the new text.

Who this is for

Learners using traditional textbooks, or reading something from Taiwan or Hong Kong, often meet a converter that assumes simplified input. Pasting traditional text here should not require a preprocessing step. Segmentation uses the dictionary’s traditional forms as well as the simplified ones, so a word written as 圖書館 can be found as a word. A rare character that is in neither form still gets a per-character reading from the pinyin library.

The same limits apply as on the Chinese to pinyin page. Citation tones are used for 一 and 不. A word with several readings lists them when you open it. You can hide pinyin by HSK level. The level lists are the same data as on the HSK pinyin page. They are not a separate traditional syllabus.

Example

銀行yínháng is “bank”. 行走xíngzǒu is “to walk”, and those two characters are the same in both scripts. 行 is háng in 銀行 and xíng in 行走.

The sample includes 銀行. Open it and the gloss should be the same bank entry you would get from 银行, because they are one dictionary record with two written forms. If a traditional-only word is missing, you will see the reading from the pinyin library and no CC-CEDICT gloss. That is an honest gap in the word list, not a failed conversion.

Questions

Will this produce Taiwanese wording?

No. 软件 becomes 軟件, not 軟體. If you need regional vocabulary, edit the words yourself. The buttons only convert characters.

Can I start from simplified and get traditional?

Yes. Paste simplified text and press 繁. Check the result. Word-level conversion is usually what you want, and it can still be wrong on a name or an ambiguous word.

Does traditional text get different pinyin?

The reading follows the word, not the script. 銀行 and 银行 share yínháng. A different word, even if it uses a related character, can have a different reading, as 行走 does.

Is Zhuyin included?

No. The readings are Hanyu Pinyin, with tone marks, tone numbers, or no tones. There is no bopomofo track.

Where is the text stored?

In this browser, if you keep it in the library. There is no account. Clearing site data removes it.
