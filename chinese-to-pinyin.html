Chinese to pinyin, word by word

This page is a Chinese to pinyin converter for a text you already have. Paste the characters and each word gets its own reading, with tone marks, sitting on that word. It is not one long string of syllables dropped under a whole paragraph. You can look a word up, hide readings you know, and print the page. It is free, it runs in the browser, and there is no account.

The tool above opens on a short sample that puts two readings of 行 next to each other. Use it as a test, or replace it with your own text from the Text button. Nothing you paste is uploaded. The library, if you save more than one text, stays in this browser.

How to convert a text

  1. Open Text, paste or type Chinese, and press Annotate with Pinyin. Shift+Enter does the same thing and closes the editor.
  2. Set the reading above the characters, below them, or inline. Tone marks are the default. Tone numbers, or no tones at all, are in Style.
  3. Click a word for the English gloss, the HSK level when the list has one, and any other reading. Switch that click to a hover in Style if you prefer.

Why the reading is attached to the word

A line of pinyin under a paragraph is hard to line up once the line wraps. PinyinMe segments the characters into words with the CC-CEDICT word list, then places the reading on that word. Spaces, line breaks, and any text that is not Chinese stay where you put them. A long passage is drawn in sections, so a chapter does not freeze the page while it is annotated.

The reading comes from the dictionary when the word is listed, and from the pinyin library otherwise. Tone sandhi for 一 and 不 is left off. If a sentence needs another reading, open the word and pick one for that spot or for the whole text.

What this converter is for

  • Self-study on a text you chose: a message, a paragraph from a book, or something you wrote.
  • Checking a word you cannot read yet, without sending the text to an account.
  • Seeing HSK 2.0 or HSK 3.0 level on a word that is on those lists, and hiding pinyin up to a level. That control is explained on the HSK pinyin page.
  • Switching the open text between simplified and traditional. See traditional Chinese to pinyin.
  • Printing a page with the readings kept on the words. See print with pinyin.

It does not make handwriting practice sheets, and it does not add Zhuyin. Definitions are short glosses from CC-CEDICT, the 12 November 2026 release, not a graded course. Listening uses a Chinese voice installed in your browser. If the device has none, playback stays unavailable.

Example: one character, two words

银行yínháng is “bank”. 行走xíngzǒu is “to walk”. 行 is háng in the first word and xíng in the second.

The sample on this page includes both words. A converter that copies a single reading of 行 onto every occurrence will get one of them wrong. PinyinMe looks up the word. When that word still has more than one reading, the dictionary lists the others and you choose. The same character in a name or a rare word may be missing. A single character that is not listed still gets a reading from the pinyin library, and you can replace it.

Questions

Does it remember my text?

Only in this browser. Save it in the library if you want it next time on this device. Clearing site data removes it. There is no login and no server copy.

Will the tones match my teacher?

Often, and not always. Citation tones are used for 一 and 不. Some words have more than one accepted reading. Open the word and pick the one this sentence needs.

Where do the definitions come from?

CC-CEDICT, used under CC BY-SA 4.0. The reader page explains how the dictionary opens: Chinese reader with a dictionary.
