Print Chinese with pinyin — paper, tones, and what is left off the page

A reading handout with pinyin

This page turns Chinese you paste into a reading handout: characters with pinyin on the same word, ready for paper or a PDF. It is not a handwriting worksheet. There are no empty boxes to trace strokes. The point is a page you can read, or hand to someone, with the pronunciation sitting on the words.

The sample above is the poem 静夜思, already set in a print-like style: pinyin above the characters, a song-style Chinese font when your device has one, and tone color turned off so the page stays quiet in black and white. Open Text if you want a different passage. Your text replaces the sample in this browser only.

How to print

  1. Paste the text and check the readings. Fix any word that is wrong for the sentence before you print.
  2. Choose Print in the toolbar. The browser’s own dialog opens, including Save as PDF. Set “Hide pinyin up to” first if you want familiar words left quiet on the paper.
  3. In that dialog, turn off the browser’s headers and footers if you do not want its URL and page number on the sheet.

What the page includes

Style, and the print section inside it, covers A4 or US Letter, portrait or landscape, and the margin in millimetres. Tones on paper can stay in color, drop to grayscale, or become underline patterns: solid, dotted, dashed, and wavy for the four tones, with no underline on the neutral tone. A small legend is included when patterns are on. The toolbar, this guide, and the buttons are left off the page. Only the reading text is printed.

Pinyin stays with its word when a line wraps. You can raise the minimum pinyin size for paper. This page selects the Print handout preset for the print dialog and uses a similar quiet layout on screen.

A handout, not a practice sheet

Pages that rank for “print Chinese with pinyin” are often stroke-practice grids. Those are useful for learning to write a character. They are a different job. PinyinMe is for reading. A learner who wants a page of a story, a song, or a poem, with readings they can follow, does not need empty boxes. If you also want to hide the pinyin on words below a level, set that before printing. Words you have tapped open stay visible. Words you have not revealed stay quiet. The HSK pinyin page describes that filter.

Simplified or traditional is whatever the text is when you press Print. The 简 and 繁 buttons rewrite the open text first if you need the other set. That conversion is described on the traditional Chinese page.

Example

明月míngyuè in the sample is “bright moon”. The line is from 静夜思, which is in the public domain. The tool adds the readings. It does not ship a library of poems.

Print the sample once before you trust a longer text. Check that the tone marks survived, that a wrapped line still keeps pinyin with its word, and that the browser did not add a header you did not want. Then paste the passage you actually need.

Questions

Can I print only the words I struggle with?

Set “Hide pinyin up to” an HSK level, or hide the most frequent words, then print. There is no way to tick individual words for the page other than revealing or hiding them in the reading view.

Does color print?

If you leave tones in color and the printer or the PDF viewer keeps color, the tone colors print. Grayscale and the underline patterns are there for a black-and-white sheet.

Will the guide and the buttons appear?

No. They are marked to drop out of print, along with the dictionary panel. If you still see a URL, it is the browser’s header. Turn that off in the print dialog.

Can I save the layout?

Style lets you save a named preset and export it. The built-in Print handout preset is the one this page selects for the print dialog. Your presets stay in this browser.

Is this a worksheet generator?

No. There are no stroke-order diagrams and no blank grids. It prints the reading text you pasted.
